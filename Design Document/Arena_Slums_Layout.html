Slum Arena Layout

One slum block, about 48 × 40 m, where gunmen keep coming. A barangay half-court in the middle, a ring of lanes around it, eight dead-end eskinitas where enemies come out of shanty doors, and a plank gallery at +4 m on the row house. Everything loops: no lane ends in a dead end for the player except the spawn alleys. Lanes are 4–6 m wide, wider than a real eskinita, so a dive has room.

The gallery is the high ground: rifle on it, sight over the whole court, but open to the court and both stairs, so standing there gets you flanked. It's a vantage point to take and then leave, not a camping spot. There's 3.6 m of headroom underneath, enough to dive through.

R1–R3 are where enemies who've lost you wander, so they cross the court and lanes looking for you instead of drifting back into the dead-end alleys.

How it plays

  • You start in the open court with enemies coming from all sides. The first move is to get out of the middle.
  • Pick a weapon route: west lane for the shotgun, the gallery for the rifle, south for the launcher.
  • Enemies arrive from doors you can't see, so you keep turning and keep circling the ring.
  • Drops from the dead keep your favourite gun fed; grenades and rockets are the scarce extras.

Layout calls I made

  • Loops everywhere. Ring lanes around the court plus the gallery's two stairs, so a pursuit can always run around rather than into a wall.
  • Enemies enter from dead ends only. Each alley ends in a shanty door, so they come out of the houses, out of view.
  • Near and far in one space: 22–24 m rifle lanes (west lane, gallery diagonal) vs. the court and stubs at shotgun range.
  • The jeepney and kiosk break the north and south lanes, which would otherwise be 48 m wall to wall.
  • Low cover is sparse and meant to dive past, never to peek from (no whack-a-mole).
